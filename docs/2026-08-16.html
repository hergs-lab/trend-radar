TikTok
What's moving on the For You Page
TK-01
Split-Screen Life Comparisons
Two routines, same Tuesday — audiences love watching the contrast without explanation.
Creators alternate quick cuts between two genuinely different daily routines (corporate vs freelance, parent vs childless, etc.) in the same visual style. The format strips out text overlays and lets the footage carry the joke, finishing on one shot that ties both lives together.
DOING IT WELL
Small creators (11K–2.9K followers) — pulling 5.3M+ views by pairing trending topics with unexpected angles
Workplace/lifestyle accounts — showing "same company, different day" contradictions
LOW-PRODUCTION RELATABLE REPEATABLE
TK-02
Searchable How-To Content
TikTok is functioning as a search engine — tight tutorials earn saves and repeat views.
Short, specific explainers compete directly with Google searches for the same question. They tend to earn more bookmarks and repeat views than most other formats because people save them to come back later. The format needs almost no production: a person, a camera, and a clear solution.
DOING IT WELL
Direct-to-camera educators — "5 things worth the money" format reads as a tip from a friend
Utility-first creators — searchable prompts with instantly legible solutions
SOCIAL SEARCH SAVES EVERGREEN
TK-03
Nostalgia Glow-Up Transitions
Back-to-school energy meets "Me at the Same Age as My Parents" — visual emotion spreads fast.
August splits between squeezing out summer content and diving into back-to-school mode. Creators are using childhood photos set to viral audio for glow-up transitions, alongside formats like "My Favorite Person" and "Seeing How My X Would Respond to a Bully" that blend nostalgia with current identity content.
DOING IT WELL
Family/parenting creators — dorm haul videos and first-day-of-school fits dominate feeds
Gen Z personal brand accounts — Paparazzi dubstep ugly-to-hot transitions and movie tie-ins
NOSTALGIC SEASONAL IDENTITY
Instagram
Trending Reels, carousels, and Stories
IG-01
Low-Polish Single-Take Skits
A single-take truck driver lip sync pulled 336M views — raw beats polished right now.
Low-production, single-take content is outperforming polished posts across Reels. Creators are embracing absurdist audio bits, performative-distress alarm clock videos, and casual formats that feel like a text from a friend rather than a produced piece. The algorithm is rewarding authenticity over production value.
DOING IT WELL
@glowsanity — posts multiple celebrity edits daily, 648K–869K views each
Casual skit creators — Netflix Documentary chair-sit format crossed from TikTok
AUTHENTIC SINGLE-TAKE ABSURDIST
IG-02
Yellow Font Vulnerability
Yellow text signals emotionally vulnerable content — or parodies the format entirely.
Based on Yellow Font Theory, creators use bold yellow captions to share personal thoughts, life lessons, or quiet realizations. The style has become shorthand for "this is my real thought" whether the sentiment is sincere or completely tongue-in-cheek. Many now parody the format by pairing yellow text with intentionally overdramatic confessions.
DOING IT WELL
Personal brand creators — using yellow for honest moments that build parasocial connection
Comedy accounts — subverting the format with absurd "confessions" in yellow
VULNERABILITY REMIXABLE TEXT-BASED
IG-03
Three-Panel Day Templates
Clean, aesthetic documentation of "What I Wore / Where I Went / What I Did" in one synced Reel.
The "What I Did" trend turns a day or trip into a clean, three-panel Reel split into horizontal sections. Each panel shows what I wore, where I went, and what I did/ate/bought, all synced to the same video. The format is simple, aesthetic, and easy to follow — perfect for documenting anything from a vacation to a casual day out.
DOING IT WELL
Lifestyle/travel creators — vacation and day-out content in clean split-screen format
Brand accounts — adapting for customer journeys, event recaps, product launches
AESTHETIC TEMPLATE BRAND-FRIENDLY
LinkedIn
What the algorithm is rewarding
LI-01
Native Documents (Carousels/PDFs)
Documents hit 6.6% engagement rate — double the performance of standard video or images.
Native document posts (carousels and PDFs) lead all formats with a 7% engagement rate, representing a 14% increase year-over-year. They align with how users consume content on LinkedIn: educational, swipeable, and easy to save. The format supports both educational content and storytelling, making it effective across different funnel stages.
DOING IT WELL
B2B educators — bite-sized value in carousel format positions them as experts
Founder/CEO accounts — sharing frameworks and industry insights in swipeable PDFs
HIGHEST ER SWIPEABLE SAVES
LI-02
Invisible Interactions (Clicks)
Clicks are doing the heavy lifting — LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" link.
While likes and comments are the most visible metrics, clicks now matter more for account authority. Average clicks per post rose from 97.54 to 102.32 (a 4.90% increase), contributing to overall engagement rates jumping from 12.21% to 13.90%. Quality and clickability now matter more than posting frequency.
DOING IT WELL
Strategic storytellers — using hooks that encourage "see more" interactions
Data-driven accounts — external links and carousel formats that generate click-throughs
CLICKABILITY AUTHORITY ENGAGEMENT
LI-03
Behind-the-Scenes Founder Content
Raw, unpolished clips outlining thought processes outperform "thought leader" polish.
Founder content is gaining traction as CEOs share personal insights, industry perspectives, and company news. Creators are posting 3-6 minute horizontal clips outlining frameworks behind their work. People are starved for simple, helpful information delivered cleanly in non-"thought leader"-type posts. LinkedIn rewards plain language in 2026 more than ever.
DOING IT WELL
Founders and CEOs — humanizing brands with personal insights and build-in-public content
Executive teams — horizontal video sharing company culture and decision frameworks
HUMAN PLAIN LANGUAGE TRUST
YouTube
Shorts, long-form, and what's compounding
YT-01
Shorts for Discovery, Long-Form for Trust
61% of organic views come from Shorts feed — use it for fast reach, then build trust with series.
More than 61% of organic views in recent data came from the Shorts feed, meaning the first job is to make ideas instantly clear, visual, and easy to grasp in seconds. Long-form still matters when it's part of a bingeable system with sequels, playlists, and clear next-watch logic that builds trust and moves viewers toward action.
DOING IT WELL
IShowSpeed — turned sports chaos into structured "Speed Goes Pro" series
Druski (Coulda Been House) — Big Brother-style format with limited-series binge appeal
DISCOVERY SERIES BINGEABLE
YT-02
Repeatable Formats (Mini Vlogs, Food, DIY)
Trending Shorts cluster around formats creators can repeat weekly without burning out.
The fastest path to trending is choosing formats you can repeat without losing quality. Mini vlogs, food content, dance, DIY, and explainers dominate because they're easy to understand, easy to rewatch, and easy to post consistently. Viewers prefer channels that offer structured arcs, recurring formats, and deeper value.
DOING IT WELL
Ghibli-style cooking channels — 10min–1hr ambient cooking ASMR with enormous session length
Faceless lifestyle channels — AI-generated village life at high volume (5 uploads/week)
REPEATABLE STRUCTURED SCALABLE
YT-03
Faceless AI-Generated Content
Channels never put the author on camera — everything runs on voiceover, stock, or AI visuals.
Four formats are doing real growth in 2026, and faceless channels are at the top. Everything runs on voiceover, screen recordings, stock footage, or AI-generated visuals. AI-generated Pakistani village life and Ghibli-style cooking content are pulling 469 views per subscriber and building deep back catalogues that keep earning from suggested views long after upload.
DOING IT WELL
AI village lifestyle channels — short, ambient, endlessly rewatchable at high production volume
Animated recipe channels — every country is a new video with the same repeatable pipeline
