TikTok

TK-01
Horror Movie Title Lists
List-style formats are crushing right now — turning everyday frustrations into fake horror movie titles with a comedic twist.
Minimal, text-driven, and highly relatable. The humor comes from how oddly specific the "horrors" are. Creators frame pet peeves, fears, or painfully relatable scenarios as worst-case storytelling. Perfect for any niche.
DOING IT WELL
Brand categories (lifestyle, SaaS, retail)
Adapting the list format to brand-specific pain points and product scenarios
Lifestyle & comedy creators
Original format drivers — mixing personal, random, specific "horror" moments
High engagement List format Relatable
TK-02
Realism Over Fantasy (#delulu decline)
Audiences are shifting from escapism to clarity — unfiltered stories, BTS moments, and real process over curated perfection are winning.
After a year of "delulu" and quiet disengagement, people crave grounding through honesty, community, and shared experience. The algorithm now rewards brands showing real people and real process. Fantasy feeds are being replaced with grounding, useful, emotional content.
DOING IT WELL
Oreo (@oreo)
Turned channel into playful clubhouse — fans riff on trends, comment culture, spontaneous content
Small business & founder accounts
Showing actual behind-the-scenes, mistakes, honest journey moments vs polished ads
Platform shift Authenticity BTS content
TK-03
"Self Aware" Audio Aesthetic
Reflective, cinematic content using the "Self Aware" audio — aesthetic visuals paired with thoughtful text overlays that feel introspective or quietly empowering.
The format is flexible: calm, confident, deep, or empowering. Creators pair beautiful visuals with meaningful text to make content feel personal and memorable. Works well for lifestyle, product demos showing life feeling easier/calmer, or reflective brand moments.
DOING IT WELL
Productivity & wellness brands
Showing peaceful routines, organized days with text about feeling more in control
Lifestyle creators (aesthetic focus)
Using cinematic b-roll with thoughtful, emotional text overlays for reflective content
Audio trend Aesthetic Emotional

Instagram

IG-01
Founder-Led Content Beats Brand Polish
People trust operators more than logos — founder-led posts with clear opinions, lessons, and real experience are outperforming polished brand content.
Instagram now acts more like a search engine. Users want fast hooks, real points of view, visible captions, and a clear next step. Corporate tone pretending to be human is fatiguing audiences. Founder content works especially well for B2B, consultants, startups, and service businesses.
DOING IT WELL
B2B founders & startup operators
Showing skin in the game, sharing real commercial insights vs content calendar filler
Service business owners
Personal takes on niche topics, lessons learned, proof of judgment over brand voice
Trust driver Founder content B2B
IG-02
On-Screen Text as Design Element
85% of scrollers watch without sound — perfectly-timed, perfectly-placed text is now a major design element, not just captions.
Text intervention following speech as a graphic element is hot. Creators use captions and on-screen text that sync with music beats or voiceover pauses. It's not just accessibility — it's creative execution. Rapid cuts (8-15 in 30 seconds) paired with synced text is the new standard.
DOING IT WELL
@manychatbr (Ana Costa)
Using text as timed graphic element, not just subtitle — design-forward approach
Product demo & tutorial creators
Timing text reveals to music beats, creating rhythm and emphasis in silent viewing
Accessibility Editing trend Mobile-first
IG-03
Shoppable Reels Expansion
Instagram expanded shoppable Reels in March 2026 — creators can now tag up to 30 products using URLs or Meta catalog items, earning affiliate commissions.
Viewers tap to see product details and pricing in-video, then go straight to buy. For brands, this means tagging products in tutorials, styling videos, comparisons, "how we use it" content — not just launches. Creator whitelisting (running ads through creator accounts) is now standard practice.
DOING IT WELL
Beauty & lifestyle creators
"Get ready with me" Reels tagging every product used, earning affiliate revenue
DTC & SaaS brands
Tagging products in non-launch content — tutorials, comparisons, styling videos
Commerce Monetization March 2026

LinkedIn

LI-01
Carousels Dominate Engagement
Carousels get 17x more interactions than images — yet images are still posted 6x more. Native documents (PDFs/carousels) lead all formats at 7% engagement.
The algorithm rewards content that keeps people on-platform. Carousels earn 1,451 impressions per post (vs 605 for video). Clicks are now a major ranking signal — LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" click. Format matters more than frequency.
DOING IT WELL
Educational creators & consultants
Breaking down frameworks, industry insights, case studies into swipeable PDF carousels
B2B SaaS brands
Repurposing blog content into native carousel documents for higher reach
Format leader Native docs High reach
LI-02
Dwell Time is King
LinkedIn wants to keep people on the platform — posts with 15+ seconds of dwell time get pushed to more feeds. Quality over quantity is survival.
The algorithm measures how long someone spends on your post vs scrolling past it. Saves are 5x more valuable than likes. The "post daily to grow" advice is outdated — the sweet spot is 2-5 high-quality posts per week. First 24 hours are critical for engagement momentum.
DOING IT WELL
Thought leaders with strong hooks
Opening lines earn the "see more" tap — using OmniCreator-style proofread tools
Story-driven founders
22x more likely to be remembered when facts wrapped in story — vulnerable posts get 8.5x engagement
Algorithm shift Dwell time Quality focus
LI-03
Raw Video Over Polished Production
Raw, unpolished behind-the-scenes video is outperforming slick production — audiences want simple, helpful information in non-thought-leadery posts.
3-6 minute horizontal clips outlining thought process or frameworks are driving engagement. People are starved for clean, helpful content. Video under 90 seconds performs best. Native uploads outperform YouTube links by 312%. The same video formats from other platforms are becoming saturated.
DOING IT WELL
Agency founders & operators
Posting 3-6 min horizontal clips showing frameworks, process — raw, helpful approach
Recruiters & service providers
60-second videos on specific tips with captions and clear CTA (e.g. "DM me for review")
Video format Authentic BTS content

YouTube

YT-01
TV-Style Serialized Content
Creators are embracing traditional TV formats — serialized videos with recurring characters, story arcs, and episodic structure for binge appeal.
Viewers prefer channels offering structured arcs, recurring formats, and deeper value. YouTube on TV is driving demand for documentary-style, episodic content. Creators like Druski (Coulda Been House, Coulda Been Love) and Glitch Studio (behind-the-scenes format) are winning with limited series that avoid viewer fatigue.
DOING IT WELL
Druski
Reality franchises (Coulda Been House/Love) — Big Brother-style format with creator chaos
Glitch Studio
Glitch Direct BTS format — casual vlog-style previews, pulls fans into production pipeline
Format shift Episodic TV viewing
YT-02
Shorts as Discovery Engine (200B daily views)
YouTube Shorts hit 200 billion average daily views in 2025 — now monetized, they're a discovery mechanism feeding long-form content and subscriptions.
Creators earn $0.03-$0.10 per 1K Shorts views (less than long-form's ~$3 RPM), but volume potential is massive. The real value is the funnel — Shorts drive discovery, then route to long-form and subscriptions. Repeatable formats (mini vlogs, food, dance, DIY, explainers) dominate because they're easy to batch and post consistently.
DOING IT WELL
Educational & tutorial creators
Using Shorts for fast value delivery, routing viewers to long-form deep dives
Food & lifestyle creators
Repeatable formats (recipe shorts, day-in-life) posted consistently for discovery volume
Monetized Discovery 200B daily
YT-03
Authentic Over Polished
Over-produced videos now underperform as viewers see them as ads — raw, unscripted, human content drives greater trust and engagement.
The algorithm prioritizes viewer satisfaction over follower count. Longer, immersive formats thrive on TV screens. Thumbnails are getting simpler, fancy edits disappearing. Creators like Sam Sulek and Dry Creek Wrangler School are shifting toward sincere, uncut videos with relaxed pacing to create intimacy with viewers.
DOING IT WELL
Sam Sulek
Unfiltered fitness content — no polish, authentic daily routines, building deep connection
Dry Creek Wrangler School
Pivoted to uncut videos sharing life lessons — meandering, sincere, intimate format
Authenticity Trust signal Long-form
