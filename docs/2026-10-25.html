TikTok
October on repeat: Halloween, text-led posts, and low-barrier dances
TK-01
Halloween Costume Reveals
Dramatic reveals set to "Ramalama (Bang Bang)" give creators a repeatable format built around anticipation and a clear payoff.
The camera holds low on legs or feet while the creator walks toward it, often dragging a prop. Then the full costume is revealed on the drum hit. No text, no talking—just structure and a recognizable audio cue.
DOING IT WELL
Halloween content creators
Using low camera angles and dragging props to build tension before the full reveal
Group costume coordinators
Layering multiple reveals in sequence to extend watch time
Seasonal Peak Audio-Driven Visual Format
TK-02
Text-Led Conversation Posts
Text setups remove production barriers and let creators rewrite around their own subject, making the format infinitely flexible.
The post opens with a conversation starter or readable hook displayed as on-screen text. The creator reacts or responds, often with minimal editing. The format works because it's reusable—change the text, keep the structure.
DOING IT WELL
Opinion-led creators
Using the "Two Fishes" audio to defend hilariously questionable takes with a straight face
Brand accounts
Adapting text prompts to call out customer habits and shortcuts people instantly recognize
High Velocity Low Barrier Reusable
TK-03
Approachable Dance & Mood Clips
Simple dances paired with chaotic captions create contrast that keeps participation open while algorithm rewards completion.
Creators perform an upbeat, easy-to-replicate dance (often to Ingrid Michaelson's "Be OK") while the caption reveals what's actually happening in their life. Happy body, chaotic text. The low skill floor means anyone can join.
DOING IT WELL
Relatable lifestyle creators
Pairing cheerful movement with brutally honest captions about everyday stress
Small business owners
Using the format to show the gap between polished brand image and messy reality
Participation Contrast Hook Completion Rate
Instagram
Carousels outpace Reels while raw content beats polish
IG-01
Vlog Timestamp Reels
One swipe-right transition per timestamp turns a full day into a mini vlog without voiceover, making the format feel personal and low-effort.
Each clip gets a visible timestamp—morning routine, breakfast, workday, evening—creating a narrative arc through structure alone. Lifestyle creators and service providers use this to make daily routines feel like serialized content.
DOING IT WELL
Lifestyle creators
Using consistent camera angles and swipe transitions to create visual rhythm
Personal brands
Layering timestamps over behind-the-scenes work to humanize their process
Series Potential Low Production Episodic
IG-02
Fan Transition Outfit Reveals
A ceiling fan cutout masks outfit changes, delivering visual satisfaction through seamless editing while showcasing multiple looks in under 15 seconds.
Creators film themselves on the floor in different outfits, clicking a remote to start. A fan cutout is overlayed and animated with keyframes, with each blade revealing a new look. Fashion creators dominate this one.
DOING IT WELL
Fashion creators
Using consistent camera positioning and lighting to make transitions feel smooth
Wardrobe stylists
Curating looks by theme (date night, work week) to add narrative structure
Visual Payoff Fashion Editing Skill
IG-03
Multi-Niche Identity Carousels
Photo dumps that rapid-fire through contradictory interests signal that refusing a single niche is itself a positioning strategy worth celebrating.
The post opens with "Refuse to be limited by a single niche," then cycles through photos representing many interests. It's made for lifestyle creators and personal brands showing there's more to them than one content lane.
DOING IT WELL
Portfolio creators
Using the format to showcase range without apology or forced cohesion
Multi-hyphenate entrepreneurs
Framing diverse skill sets as a strength rather than a branding problem
Anti-Niche Carousel Positioning
LinkedIn
Carousels dominate while personal profiles leave company pages behind
LI-01
Document Carousels
Carousels earn 1.39x reach and drive engagement through swiping behavior that signals high dwell time to the algorithm.
Document posts outperform every other format on the platform, including video which crashed to 0.86x reach. Only 4.9% of profiles use carousels regularly, leaving a wide open opportunity. Multi-image posts boast 3.71% engagement rate—double that of video.
DOING IT WELL
B2B thought leaders
Structuring carousels around frameworks and step-by-step breakdowns that reward attention
Marketing agencies
Repurposing long-form content into swipeable insights with clear takeaways per slide
Top Format Dwell Time Underused
LI-02
Employee-Generated Content
People trust people more than logos—posts from employees, founders, and sales teams earn 8x more engagement than company pages.
Personal profiles generate 2.60% engagement versus 0.91x for company pages. Employee posts feel authentic and spark genuine conversations. A sales manager sharing lessons from closing a deal outperforms the company's promotional content every time.
DOING IT WELL
Startup founders
Sharing unfiltered lessons learned and founder struggles that company pages can't voice
Customer success teams
Posting real client stories and problem-solving journeys with specific outcomes
Trust Signal Personal > Brand Authenticity
LI-03
Short Punchy Text Posts
Posts under 100 words with a strong hook get read faster and drive quicker engagement in a feed designed for scroll speed.
Text posts with clear formatting (line breaks, bold text, lists) make longer content scannable while packing in specific insights. The algorithm rewards depth—comments are worth 8x more than likes for reach. End with a question to spark discussion.
DOING IT WELL
Content strategists
Opening with a contrarian take, then backing it with one sharp example
Industry commentators
Writing "meta-content" about LinkedIn itself, which generates 61% more reach than other topics
Fast Read Hook-First Conversation Driver
YouTube
Shorts drive discovery while challenge formats dominate revenue
YT-01
Day-in-the-Life Mini Vlogs
Mini vlogs tell a complete story in under 60 seconds with context, motion, and payoff—making them the default comfort format across niches.
The structure is familiar: creator life, student routines, small business days, travel clips, gym progress. They work because viewers know what to expect and the format is endlessly repeatable. Shorts under 60 seconds earn 2x higher completion rates.
DOING IT WELL
Student creators
Using timestamps and consistent locations to build episodic rhythm
Small business owners
Showing behind-the-scenes work with quick cuts that maintain pacing
Discovery Engine Completion Rate Repeatable
YT-02
High-Stakes Challenge Videos
Challenge content leads October revenue through repeatable stakes and participation—viewers stay to see the outcome, and clear constraints make the format shareable.
Challenges earned $589K in estimated October revenue. The format works through bounded uncertainty and visible transformation. Prize-led challenges, philanthropy reveals, and participation-driven content cluster around clear stakes and outcomes.
DOING IT WELL
MrBeast-style creators
Building visible stakes and clear time constraints that create tension
Gaming channels
Layering community speculation and update reactions into challenge formats
Revenue Leader Stakes Transformation
YT-03
Quick DIY & Tutorial Shorts
DIY Shorts trend when they remove friction—viewers love "I can do that" content with a clear payoff and no barrier to replication.
Home fixes, phone tricks, editing shortcuts, desk setups, and budget upgrades perform because they deliver tiny transformations. The strongest DIY Shorts feel achievable and useful, driving both rewatches and saves.
DOING IT WELL
Tech tip channels
Opening with the problem solved, then showing the three-step process
Home improvement creators
Using before-and-after structure with no voiceover needed
Utility Rewatch Value Low Barrier
Always in the mix
