TikTok

TK-01
Self Aware Audio — Reflective Cinematic Content
Aesthetic visuals paired with thoughtful text overlays are driving emotional engagement without heavy production.
The "Self Aware" audio by Temper City has 434K+ videos and is powering dreamy, introspective content. Creators layer meaningful text over cinematic b-roll, tapping into "realism over fantasy" sentiment. The format is flexible enough for calm, confident, or empowering tones, and TikTok's 2026 algorithm rewards grounded, relatable storytelling that helps users feel present rather than distracted.
Doing it well
Lifestyle & Wellness Creators
Showing peaceful routines or organized days with text about clarity and control.
Brand Storytellers
Using the audio for product moments that show life feeling easier or more intentional.
Authenticity Cinematic Realism
TK-02
Top 5 Horror Movies — Relatable List Format
Text-driven lists of "real-life horrors" are turning mundane anxieties into highly shareable comedy.
This minimal, text-only format starts like a ranking but lists oddly specific, relatable situations instead of actual movies. The humor comes from how personal and unhinged the "horrors" feel. It's working because people relate to niche anxieties presented in an absurd way. Brands can insert "life before our product" into the list without feeling forced. The trend taps into self-aware comedy that TikTok's 2026 algorithm favors over polished content.
Doing it well
Comedy & Commentary Creators
Listing random anxieties like "waking up to 47 unread emails" in horror-movie format.
Product-Based Brands
Sneaking "before using [product]" moments into relatable horror lists.
Relatability Comedy Text-driven
TK-03
Loving Life Again — Soft Reset Content
Small transformations and "life is lighter" moments are resonating over big, dramatic glow-ups.
Ella Langley's "Loving Life Again" is fueling soft, positive content showing the moment life feels better after a small reset. It's not about massive change—it's about removing friction. Creators show progress, a glow-up, or returning to balance. The tone is reflective, making it ideal for brands showing how a product improved someone's routine. TikTok's 2026 focus on "grounding" over escapism makes this particularly effective right now.
Doing it well
Fitness & Lifestyle Creators
Showing consistent routines or hitting a goal with the "life feels lighter" framing.
Travel & Experience Brands
Destination content framed as "finally doing the thing I should have done."
Transformation Audio-led Positive

Instagram

IG-01
Split-Screen Process vs Result
Behind-the-scenes mess next to polished output is earning saves because it shows the real work behind the gloss.
This format contrasts the messy process with the finished product in a split-screen layout. It works because audiences crave transparency and the glow-up is instantly satisfying. Think shoot BTS beside the edited Reel, or a GRWM outfit layout next to the final look. Instagram's algorithm in April 2026 is rewarding authenticity and "rawness," making this format especially effective for photographers, creators, and product brands showing real production.
Doing it well
Photographers & Creatives
Showing raw shoot conditions vs final retouched images with moody editing.
Food & Product Brands
Chaotic prep scenes beside beautifully plated or styled final shots.
Authenticity BTS Transformation

Tame Impala Cinematic Aesthetic
Moody, editorial-style content is thriving on Instagram—no explanation needed, just vibe.
Tame Impala tracks are having a moment on Reels in April 2026. Creators are pairing the moody, cinematic energy with aesthetic-forward content—portfolio teasers, process footage, and editorial product shots that prioritize feel over information. The format works for luxury brands, photographers, and real estate because it lets the visuals and audio do the storytelling. Instagram's algorithm is favoring high dwell time, and this format keeps people watching.
Doing it well
Fashion & Luxury Brands
Close-up textures, slow movement, dark color palettes set to cinematic audio.
Real Estate Creators
Luxury property walkthroughs with no voiceover—just architecture and music.
Cinematic Moody High Dwell Time
IG-03
World Stop! Transformation Format
One-take before-and-after videos are exploding because they deliver instant visual payoff.
The "World Stop!" audio by @browsbyzulema is the breakout transformation format heading into April 2026. It's a single-take glow-up that works across beauty, fashion, fitness, and home content. The audio mechanic creates a dramatic pause before the reveal, keeping viewers watching. Instagram's algorithm rewards high completion rates, and this format delivers. Brands can use it for product reveals, makeovers, or any visual transformation with clear before/after appeal.

LinkedIn

LI-01
Native Documents Dominate Engagement
Carousel PDFs are hitting 7% engagement rates—more than double what video gets on LinkedIn right now.
LinkedIn's algorithm in 2026 is heavily rewarding native document posts (carousels and PDFs). They earn 1,451 average impressions per post, far surpassing video's 605. Documents drive "invisible" engagement—clicks and saves—which LinkedIn tracks as high-value signals. Brands should repurpose blogs, case studies, and frameworks into carousel format. The format works because it encourages swipes and dwell time, both key ranking factors. Multi-image posts are posted far less than video, creating a blue ocean opportunity.
Doing it well
B2B Thought Leaders
Turning frameworks and best practices into 5-10 slide carousels with clear takeaways.
Marketing & Consulting Brands
Repurposing case studies and whitepapers into visually clean carousel documents.
Carousels High Engagement Dwell Time
LI-02
Raw Behind-the-Scenes Video
Unpolished, horizontal-format clips outlining thought processes are outperforming slick video on LinkedIn.
LinkedIn audiences in 2026 are starved for simple, helpful information delivered in a non-thought-leadery way. Three to six-minute horizontal clips that feel like a casual walkthrough or framework breakdown are driving strong engagement. The key is raw authenticity—no fancy editing, just useful insight. This format works because it counters over-edited content and AI-generated posts by feeling genuinely human. Social Insider's 2026 data shows creators and brands posting this style are seeing significantly higher engagement.
Doing it well
Agency Founders & Consultants
Screen-recording walkthroughs or casual talking-head frameworks with no polish.
Service-Based Businesses
Sharing client process or real project examples in a candid, BTS format.
Authenticity Long-form BTS
LI-03
Dwell Time Over Vanity Metrics
LinkedIn's algorithm now weights saves and time-on-post 5x higher than likes—quality beats quantity.
LinkedIn's 2026 algorithm prioritizes dwell time (15+ seconds) and saves over likes and comments. Posts that make people stop scrolling and actually read are pushed to more feeds. The "post daily" advice is outdated—2 to 5 high-quality posts per week is the new sweet spot. Comment quality also trumps quantity: 20 thoughtful comments beat 200 emoji reactions. Brands should focus on content that's worth saving and sharing, not just content that racks up quick engagement. Strong hooks and substantive value are non-negotiable.
Doing it well
Strategic Personal Brands
Posting 3x per week with hooks that earn the "see more" tap and long-form insight.
B2B SaaS Companies
Sharing real results, case studies, and frameworks that get saved for later reference.
Algorithm Shift Saves Quality Content

YouTube

YT-01
Recurring Series Over One-Offs
Creators building episodic formats are growing faster because viewers crave structure and want to come back for more.
YouTube's 2026 data shows audiences prefer channels offering structured arcs and recurring formats over random uploads. Think reality-style series, weekly commentary shows, or multi-part explainers. Creators like Druski with "Coulda Been House" and Quenlin Blackwell's "Feeding Starving Celebrities" are proving that digestible series (under 10 episodes) with clear hooks outperform single viral videos. The format builds habit and loyalty, which YouTube's algorithm rewards with higher subscriber conversion and watch time.
Doing it well
Druski
Reality franchises like Coulda Been House attract 710K viewers with familiar structure and chaos.
Glitch Studio
Glitch Direct series previews upcoming animation in a casual, transparent vlog style.
Series Format Recurring Binge Appeal
YT-02
Authentic Over Polished
Raw, unscripted content is outperforming highly produced videos—audiences want intimacy, not perfection.
Over-produced videos now often underperform because viewers see them as ads. Creators like Sam Sulek and Dry Creek Wrangler School are thriving with uncut, sincere videos that share life lessons or daily routines with minimal editing. YouTube Shorts are now getting 90 billion views daily, but long-form authentic content is holding TV-watching audiences. The key is creating intimacy and trust through real conversation and behind-the-scenes moments, not through cinematic production value.
Doing it well
Sam Sulek
Unedited gym sessions and life reflections with relaxed pacing and genuine storytelling.
Mission-Driven Organizations
Field updates, self-recorded team moments, and unscripted behind-the-scenes context.
Authenticity Unscripted Low Production
YT-03
Dual Format Strategy: Long + Short
Creators mixing YouTube Shorts with long-form content are unlocking discovery and depth in one workflow.
YouTube in 2026 rewards creators who use both formats strategically. Shorts (15-45 seconds) drive discovery with fast hooks and satisfying payoff. Long-form videos (8-20 minutes) reward viewers who choose to stay. The platform's algorithm prioritizes viewer satisfaction over follower count, and longer, immersive formats are thriving on TV screens. Creators should shoot vertical for Shorts, repurpose long content into cutdowns, and think in terms of content series, not one-offs. Multi-format workflows are now the norm.
Doing it well
Educational Channels
Publishing explainer Shorts for discovery, then offering deep-dive long-form for engaged viewers.
Product & Tech Brands
Using Shorts for feature highlights and long-form for tutorials, comparisons, and storytelling.
