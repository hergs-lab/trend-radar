TikTok
What's moving on the For You Page
TK-01
Niche Specificity in Humor
The more painfully specific the situation, the better the joke performs — TikTok's algorithm now rewards ultra-targeted relatability over broad appeal.
The "I Try to Act Normal" trend demonstrates how far specificity can take a simple format. Creators describe outwardly calm situations where they're internally losing it, but the caption carries all the weight. Brands that make every observation universally relatable are losing ground to those who nail specific audience micro-moments.
DOING IT WELL
@nikki_rodriguez
Uses "I Try to Act Normal" audio with hyper-specific team dynamics that resonate with niche audiences.
Social agencies & B2B brands
Joking about "make the logo bigger" moments that feel like inside jokes to the right audience.

TK-02
Teaching Content & Longer Short-Form
Educational formats with clear structure and single-topic focus are the safest bet on TikTok in 2026 — viewers want quick lessons with real results.
TikTok is rewarding longer short-form videos (90+ seconds), search-optimized content, and teaching formats that keep viewers watching longer. The strongest teaching videos have one clear goal, explain it well, then end. This structure builds trust and drives saves. Format-first comedy is being balanced by substance-driven content.
DOING IT WELL
Fitness & tutorial creators
Delivering concise form tips, camera setups, and interview advice with teleprompter-level clarity.

TK-03
Delayed Realization Format
Hindsight-driven storytelling is pulling 2M+ likes — creators reveal something they completely missed at the time, with the audio landing as it finally clicks.
The format runs on embarrassment and hindsight. You put a memory on screen (usually something cringeworthy you missed), and the "Yartrix" audio hits at the exact moment of realization. The versions that travel are specific and slightly embarrassing. It suits brands well because delayed-realization framing lets you tell the joke without pointing at a customer.
DOING IT WELL
@Yartrix (audio creator)
Original audio powering 2.4M-like videos about realizing someone was throwing shade years ago.
Industry insiders
Sharing "everybody quietly understood except me" moments that feel authentic and industry-specific.

Instagram
What's gaining traction on Reels and carousels
IG-01
DM Shares Over Likes
A post sent to 10 relevant people beats 100 polite likes — DM shares now carry 3-5x the algorithmic weight of likes in 2026 distribution.
CreatorFlow's Instagram trend analysis reveals DM shares are far stronger distribution signals than likes. A save means "I may need this later." A DM send means "This is relevant to someone right now" — closer to a recommendation than applause. Founders should treat content as a useful object passed between people, not a stage performance.
DOING IT WELL
Startup & SaaS founders
Creating sendable, useful Reels with hard numbers and specific takeaways instead of vague affirmations.
Educational carousel accounts
Posting swipeable design earning 3-5x the save/share rate of single images with DM-worthy insights.

IG-02
Potential-Maxxing With Hard Numbers
Creators are backing up self-improvement claims with real metrics — post counts, view totals, revenue — instead of vague inspirational language.
The "Potential-Maxxing" trend has creators proving they're unlocking potential with data: follower growth, content output numbers, and measurable results. It's a reaction against empty affirmations. The format uses original audio (no licensing lock) and works across niches. Audiences respond to visible proof over aspirational vibes.
DOING IT WELL
@mean.ceo community
Tracking output metrics and sharing transparent progress updates with actual view/revenue data.
Productivity & growth creators
Replacing "trust the process" captions with "I posted 47 times in 30 days, here's what changed."

IG-03
First-in-Bloodline Comedy Format
Taking completely normal achievements and framing them like historic milestones — the more dramatic the wording versus the actual event, the funnier it lands.
This carousel trend lets creators announce mundane life upgrades with exaggerated gravitas: "First in my bloodline to quit her job and become a full-time creator." The gap between the achievement and the phrasing is the joke. It's relatable because it mocks the pressure to make everything sound revolutionary while celebrating small, genuine wins.
DOING IT WELL
Millennial & Gen Z creators
Pairing dramatic captions with photos of genuinely impressive or hilariously trivial life updates.
Lifestyle & career accounts
Using the format to celebrate career pivots, side hustles, or small daily victories with self-aware humor.

LinkedIn
What the algorithm is rewarding now
LI-01
Document Posts (PDF Carousels)
Native documents are crushing every other format with 6.6-7% engagement rates — more than triple the performance of standard text posts.
LinkedIn's 360Brew AI ranking system heavily favors document posts (PDF carousels). They encourage swiping, which counts as an invisible interaction boosting engagement rates. Carousels earn 1,451 impressions per post vs. 605 for video. The format supports educational content and storytelling, making it effective across funnel stages. Plain-language, non-corporate tone performs best.
DOING IT WELL
B2B thought leaders
Publishing frameworks, industry breakdowns, and case studies in swipeable PDF format with data-rich slides.
Marketing agencies
Sharing client results and process walkthroughs in multi-slide carousels that drive saves and shares.

LI-02
Raw Behind-the-Scenes Video
Unpolished, 3-6 minute horizontal clips outlining thought processes and frameworks are earning higher engagement than polished corporate video.
Video content is up 36% YoY, but the winning format isn't slick production. LinkedIn audiences respond to raw, behind-the-scenes-feeling posts: horizontal clips showing real work, frameworks, and simple helpful information. The lack of "thought-leadery" polish is the appeal. Video grew 27% in creation and 36% in views because authenticity beats production value.
DOING IT WELL
Sales & ops professionals
Recording phone-shot walkthroughs of their approach to deals, hiring, and internal systems.
Founders & consultants
Posting 3-6 minute talking-head videos explaining frameworks without scripts or heavy editing.

LI-03
Clicks as Hidden Engagement
LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" click — these invisible interactions now drive engagement rates more than comments.
Clicks per post rose 4.9% (97 to 102 average), driving overall engagement rates from 12.2% to 13.9% even as posting frequency dropped 10%. Quality and "clickability" now matter more than volume. The algorithm rewards content that makes people interact beyond passive scrolling. Multi-image posts hit 3.71% ER, double that of video or single images.
DOING IT WELL
Data-driven marketers
Structuring posts with strong hooks that force users to click "see more" to get the full insight.
Multi-image content creators
Using 3-5 image posts with compelling progression that demands clicking through each slide.

YouTube
What's working on Shorts and long-form
YT-01
Shorts for Discovery, Long for Trust
Shorts win discovery at 200B daily views, but long videos, livestreams, and explainers build repeat viewing and buyer action — smart channels run both in parallel.
YouTube CEO Neal Mohan confirmed Shorts hit 200B average daily views in 2025, reshaping discovery. But the winning strategy uses Shorts to spark interest, then funnels viewers to longer demos, product walkthroughs, and live sessions. Epidemic Sound's 2026 report highlights real-life moments, creator crossovers, and longer videos as major patterns. Small focused channels win by serving one audience deeply.
DOING IT WELL
SaaS & product channels
Using Shorts for attention-grabbing problem statements, linking to 8-15 min solution walkthroughs.
Educational creators
Posting snackable Shorts daily while building trust with weekly deep-dive tutorials and live Q&As.

YT-02
Repeatable Format Structures
Trending Shorts cluster around fast comprehension, strong retention, and rewatch loops — formats creators can publish weekly without burning out.
The 2026 Shorts trend isn't a single topic, it's repeatable structures: mini vlogs, food, dance, DIY, explainers. These formats dominate because they're easy to understand, easy to rewatch, and easy to post consistently. The trend is the structure, not the subject. Creators who pick 2-3 formats matching their niche and posting cadence see sustained growth without content fatigue.
DOING IT WELL
DIY & tutorial creators
Posting the same format structure weekly with different projects, maintaining quality and predictability.
Food & lifestyle channels
Using mini-vlog structures for recipe reveals and getting-ready moments that audiences recognize instantly.

YT-03
Interactive & Live Formats
2026 is giving more weight to formats allowing real-time participation — live streams with Q&A modules, watch parties, and shoppable overlays are rising fast.
Social media research highlights organized live streams with news recaps, office hours, co-commentary, and interactive tools (surveys, Q&A, clickable overlays, chapter markers). Live is useful for testing ideas quickly — concepts that resonate can become refined edited pieces. Shoppable video formats (live demos, product listings, affiliate groups) are becoming mainstream, not experimental.
DOING IT WELL
Gaming & commentary channels
Running co-watch parties with live chat, polls, and real-time reactions that drive sustained engagement.
Product review channels
Adding shoppable links and affiliate overlays during live demos, converting viewers in real-time.
