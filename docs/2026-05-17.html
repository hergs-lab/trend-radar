TikTok

Niche-First, Algorithm-Legible Formats

TK-01

Repeatable Formats Win Distribution

73% of views come from For You—so the algorithm needs to categorize you quickly.

Replicable formats signal to TikTok's system what niche you occupy, making placement easier. Creators using consistent series structures (same opening, same hook pattern, same pacing) are seeing better sustained reach than one-off viral attempts.

Niching Down Algorithm Literacy Series Over Singles
TK-02

Playful Defensiveness Trend

"No, you don't get to ask questions" is the hook—chaos without explanation is the comedy.

The format shows creators doing something questionable and refusing to clarify. Tension drives humor: something doesn't add up, but instead of explaining, they shut it down. Works across wellness, product demos, and lifestyle niches where the "why" is intentionally withheld.

SaaS/app brands

Short Window Audio-Led Relatable Chaos
TK-03

Gratitude Format: "May This Month"

Seasonal framing makes content feel timely—but the gratitude mechanic is endlessly adaptable.

Creators use "May this month [insert gratitude]" to connect emotionally with audiences through simple, feel-good moments. It's low production but high resonance. Easy to brand-adapt: hospitality, wellness, finance, or community sectors can layer in product/service moments without disrupting the format's warmth.

Doing it well
Lifestyle & wellness creators
Pair gratitude voiceover with b-roll of daily routines or products in use
Community-focused brands
Highlighting team wins, customer stories, or values without overt selling
Emotional ROI Seasonal Hook Low Production
Instagram

Cinematic Lists, Creative Confessionals & Audio Momentum

IG-01

Bieber's "You're So Creative" Confessional

Polished work stitched with chaotic process = millennial catnip.

The format: show final deliverable with "You're so creative!" overlay, then hard cut to the 47 takes, midnight panic, or creative chaos behind it. At 130K+ Reels, it's at peak—move fast. Works for designers, photographers, content creators, freelancers, and agencies ready to humanize the hustle.

Doing it well
Design studios
Polished brand work → chaotic Figma/Slack process behind it
Photographers
Beautiful final shot → reveal of 200 takes to nail it
Peak Trend Behind-the-Scenes Millennial Humor
IG-02

"Everything Hallelujah" List Format

Justin Bieber's Coachella return sent this to the top—list your wins, each followed by "hallelujah."

The repetition creates satisfying rhythm. Each "hallelujah" punctuates like a mic drop. Visit Austin TX used it for city highlights ("live music capital, hallelujah. MICHELIN barbecue, hallelujah"). Works for destination marketing, product features, self-care routines, and brand storytelling where cumulative effect > single claim.

IG-03

"AI Is Going to Take Your Job" Comedy

Film yourself doing unglamorous, physical, chaotic work → overlay "AI is going to take your job" → cut to "WHEN?!"

The AI takeover discourse has been reclaimed for comedy. Creators in trades, hospitality, manual labor, childcare, and creative fields are using this to showcase irreplaceable human effort with humor. Relatable, cathartic, and weirdly comforting. Works for any role where the messiness can't be automated.

Doing it well
Tradespeople & manual workers
Showcasing physical, hands-on tasks AI can't replicate
Hospitality & service workers
Highlighting chaotic, human-centered moments in customer service

LinkedIn

Carousels, Clicks & Thought Leadership

LI-01

Carousels Dominate Reach & Saves

Carousels earn 1,451 avg impressions vs 605 for video—and 2.38 comments per post.

LinkedIn's algorithm rewards dwell time and saves. Carousels guide readers through a clear idea from start to finish—frameworks, breakdowns, step-by-step insights. They generate more saves and shares, which extends reach over time. Multi-image posts boast 3.71% engagement vs 1.80% for video.

Doing it well
Marketing agencies
Framework carousels (5-7 slides) explaining processes or strategies
Thought leaders
Data-driven insights structured as visual story arcs
Format Leader High Saves Educational
LI-02

Clicks Are the Invisible Engagement Driver

Between 2025-2026, avg clicks per post rose 4.9%—contributing to 13.90% engagement rate (up from 12.21%).

LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" click. These actions factor into your engagement rate. Quality and clickability now matter more than posting frequency (which dropped 10% YoY). 50% of impressions happen in first 48 hours—timing and hooks are critical.

Doing it well
B2B SaaS brands
Posts with external links to case studies or tools—optimized for click intent
Consultants
Use polls and carousels to drive interaction without leaving platform
Algorithm Insight Engagement Rate 48-Hour Window
LI-03

Text Posts Still Lead for Engagement

51% of users are most likely to interact with text posts from brands—more than video (27%).

Video is growing (watch time up 36% YoY), but LinkedIn's audience still prioritizes educational, text-based insights. The content that performs best teaches something useful, reframes thinking, or shares a clear POV based on real experience. Depth matters more than polish.

Doing it well
Founders & executives
Personal stories with lessons learned—authentic, unpolished, human
Industry analysts
Clear commentary on industry shifts with sharp takeaways
Text-First Thought Leadership Authenticity
YouTube

Episodic Content, Shorts Funnel & TV Viewing

YT-01

Serialized Episodic Formats

Viewers crave structure: recurring formats, character arcs, and binge-worthy series over one-offs.

Quenlin Blackwell's "Feeding Starving Celebrities" grew from 10-min episodes to hour-long slots. Druski's "Coulda Been House" reality franchise hit 710K subs in a year. Glitch Studio's "Glitch Direct" pulls fans into production pipeline. Serialized content builds loyalty, anticipation, and repeat viewing—outperforming standalone viral attempts.

Doing it well
Quenlin Blackwell
"Feeding Starving Celebrities"—humor-driven interview format, hour-long episodes
Glitch Studio
"Glitch Direct" behind-the-scenes vlog series for animation fans
Recurring Formats Binge Appeal Loyalty Builder
YT-02

Shorts as Discovery Funnel

Shorts crossed 70B daily views—they're the top-of-funnel that feeds long-form subscriptions.

Creators earn $0.03–$0.10 per 1K Shorts views (vs ~$3 RPM for long-form), but volume is massive. Real story: Shorts drive discovery. Viewers find you in 60 seconds, then subscribe for deep dives. Madeline Argy built Gen Z following with raw 40-min reflections after Shorts introduced her voice.

Doing it well
Madeline Argy
Shorts for discovery → long-form confessionals for depth and loyalty
Explainer channels
Use Shorts for hooks, long-form for nuance and ad revenue
Discovery Engine Two-Track Strategy Monetization Funnel
YT-03

TV Watchers Shift Viewing Behavior

YouTube on TV = intentional sit-down viewing—longer ads resonate, cinematic storytelling thrives.

The rise of "TV watchers" marks shift from passive scrolling to intentional consumption. Longer-form, story-led videos hold attention and deepen engagement. YouTube's living-room audience seeks compelling, cinematic storytelling—not quick hits. Brands should use two-track format strategy: long-form for TV, Shorts for mobile.
