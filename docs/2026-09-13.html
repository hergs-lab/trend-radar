TikTok
TK-01
Hyper-Specific Niche Comedy
The caption does all the work—the more painfully specific the scenario, the harder it lands.
Formats like "I Try to Act Normal" rely on specificity over universality. The audio is secondary; captions describe oddly recognisable situations that feel like an inside joke—but to thousands of people at once. Brands win when they stop chasing mass appeal and lean into the uncomfortable specificity their audience actually experiences.
Doing it well
@pepperagency (meta-trend example) — Demonstrated "shoes off, another pub?" format success
Social agencies using workplace humour — "Make the logo bigger" jokes target tight professional niches
Signal Format-first Caption comedy
TK-02
Delayed-Realisation Hindsight
Memory meets punchline—the sound drops exactly when the penny finally drops.
This trend runs on hindsight. Creators pair "original sound – Yartrix" with a memory they completely missed at the time. The realisation clicks years later. One video sits at 2.4M likes with nothing more than belated shade recognition. Brands can use this to spotlight product misunderstandings or industry knowledge customers wish they'd known sooner—without pointing directly at the customer.
Doing it well
@yartrix (audio originator) — Created the viral sound driving 2.4M+ like videos
Relationship/workplace storytellers — Use delayed-realisation framing for embarrassing hindsight moments
Viral audio Storytelling Nostalgia
TK-03
Emotional ROI Verification
TikTok is now a buyer research hub—audiences verify before they purchase.
TikTok's 2026 trend report identifies "Emotional ROI" as a core signal: buyers use the platform as a verification hub before purchasing. This shifts content strategy from fantasy to realism and curiosity-driven discovery. Users want proof, not polish. Demos, unboxings, honest reviews, and real-world testing formats now outperform aspirational content. The For You Page is a pre-purchase search engine.
Doing it well
Unboxing/review creators — Honest product testing satisfies buyer verification behaviour
Process-driven content (e.g., arceriinteriors) — Behind-the-scenes realism builds trust before transaction
Platform shift Social commerce Buyer intent
Instagram
Instagram
IG-01
Bedazzle Everything Maximalism
Rhinestones, gems, charms, crystals—on products, skin, desks, anything—whimsical chaos wins.
Creators are slapping decorative elements on everything. If you're in beauty, fashion, hair, nails, or lifestyle, this is permission to go full maximalist. The format works because it's tactile, transformative, and visually arresting in a feed full of beige minimalism. Original audio from creator accounts means no licensing friction. It's playful, repeatable, and perfect for product-led content with personality.
Doing it well
Beauty/lifestyle creators (trend category) — Using "Bedazzle Everything" audio for product decoration content
Nail/accessories accounts — Showcasing whimsical gem/charm application on wearables
Visual trend Maximalism Product-led
IG-02
Potential-Maxxing With Numbers
Vague affirmations are out—hard numbers prove you're unlocking your potential.
Creators back up self-improvement claims with metrics: post counts, view totals, follower growth, income milestones. This is "show your work" content. No licensing locks on the audio means wide adoption. The trend reflects audience fatigue with vague motivational content and a preference for receipts. For brands, this means transparency around process, growth, and results—not just aspirational imagery.
Doing it well
Growth-focused creators — Sharing milestone screenshots with exact metrics as proof
Fitness/education accounts — Documenting progress with before/after data, not just images
Transparency Data-driven Self-improvement
IG-03
DM Shares Trump Likes
Instagram's algorithm weights DM shares 3-5x higher than likes for Reels distribution.
Adam Mosseri confirmed the three most important ranking factors: watch time, likes per reach, and DM shares—with DM shares carrying the most weight for reaching new audiences. A send is a trust signal. When someone shares your Reel to a friend, they're vouching for it. The algorithm interprets this as high intent and rewards it with broader distribution. 100 likes matter less than 10 DM shares.
Doing it well
Meme/relatable content creators — Designing "send to a friend" moments into every Reel
Educational carousel accounts — Creating save/share-worthy frameworks people want to reference later
Algorithm shift DM shares Distribution
LinkedIn
LinkedIn
LI-01
Native Documents Dominate
Carousels and PDFs earn 7% engagement—14% higher than video and double image posts.
Native document posts (carousels/PDFs) lead all formats with a 7% engagement rate, far surpassing video at 1.80% and single images at 1.81%. They also earn 1,451 average impressions per post versus 605 for video. The format supports educational content and storytelling while aligning with how LinkedIn users consume on desktop. Carousels work because they encourage swiping—an invisible engagement signal the algorithm tracks and rewards.
Doing it well
B2B educators/consultants — Using carousel format for frameworks and case studies
Thought leaders with data — Turning insights into swipeable, screenshot-worthy PDFs
Format leader Carousels Swipe signal
LI-02
Invisible Clicks Matter More
Clicks—not likes or comments—are doing the heavy lifting for your account authority.
Between 2025 and 2026, average clicks per post rose from 97.54 to 102.32, a 4.90% increase. Overall engagement rates jumped from 12.21% to 13.90%. LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" click. These invisible interactions factor into your engagement rate even when there's no visible reaction. Quality and clickability now matter more than posting frequency. The algorithm rewards content that keeps people on platform.
Doing it well
Long-form storytellers — Crafting hooks that force "see more" clicks to finish the narrative
Data-rich accounts — Leading with insight teases that require click-through for full context
Hidden metric Clickability Engagement
LI-03
Raw Over Corporate Polish
Unpolished behind-the-scenes posts outperform "thought-leadery" content—people want helpful, not polished.
Raw, unpolished behind-the-scenes content is driving higher engagement than overly polished corporate messages. One creator reports posting 3-6 minute horizontal clips outlining thought processes or frameworks with excellent results. LinkedIn rewards plain language in 2026 more than ever. Audiences are tired of the same hook/insight/CTA structure saturating the feed. They want simple, helpful information cleanly presented—not performative thought leadership.
Doing it well
Founders sharing process — Posting horizontal video walkthroughs of real work, not highlight reels
Employee-generated content — Authentic posts from team members consistently outperform brand voice
Authenticity BTS content Plain language
YouTube
YouTube
YT-01
Shorts as Discovery Layer
Shorts reach 200B daily views—use them to spark interest, then move viewers to long-form.
YouTube Shorts hit 200 billion average daily views in 2025, reshaping creator discovery. Shorts now function as the discovery layer—fast comprehension, strong retention, rewatch loops. But long videos, livestreams, and deep dives build trust, repeat viewing, and buyer action. The winning strategy: use Shorts to spark interest in your niche, then funnel viewers to longer demos, explainers, live sessions, or walkthroughs that show your method and answer real buyer questions.
Doing it well
Educational creators with dual formats — Posting Shorts teases that link to full tutorials in descriptions
Product review channels — Using Shorts for quick takes, long-form for detailed walkthroughs
Format strategy Discovery funnel Short + long
YT-02
Experiment-Format Retention
The viewer wants to know the outcome—structured experiments keep people watching to the end.
Experiment formats have one huge advantage: viewers want to know the result. "I lived for a week spending only $10 a day. Here's what happened" beats "5 ways to save money" because it creates a specific question: Did it work? Challenges, tests, and hypothesis-experiment-result structures remain powerful for viral YouTube content in 2026. The format works across niches—adapt popular challenges to your vertical rather than copying them exactly.
Doing it well
Challenge/experiment creators — Structuring videos around testable hypotheses with clear outcomes
Niche adaptation accounts — Taking viral challenge formats and translating them to specific industries
Watch time Retention hook Structured story
YT-03
Cultural Relevance Over Scale
Global attention is fragmented, not weak—niche focus beats broad entertainment for small channels.
Early September trending data shows an unusually broad mix: Counter-Strike broadcasts, Indian film teasers, Roblox videos, BIGBANG music, GTA reactions. The pattern is clear: global attention is fragmented, not weak. This fragmentation offers a serious opening for smaller channels. You don't need to beat a global celebrity in a generic category. You need to serve one defined audience better than broad entertainment can. September is a strong month for useful content and recurring formats as people return to routine.
Doing it well
IShowSpeed — Turned sports chaos into structured "Speed Goes Pro" series, gained 11-17M subs
SleepyCrafter — Built cult fanbase with 3D-printing hide-and-seek niche content
Niche strategy Cultural relevance Recurring formats
