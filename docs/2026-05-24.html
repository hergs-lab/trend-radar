Platform 01
TikTok
TK-01
"And Emily... That's All" Audio Wave
The Devil Wears Prada 2 hit theaters May 1st and Miranda Priestly's deadpan dismissal became the dominant audio within days.
Fashion, beauty, and lifestyle creators are using it for vibe-contrast skits showing Miranda-approved content versus behind-the-scenes chaos. The format pairs elevated aesthetics with workplace humor, running through at least May 20 while the film stays in theaters.
Doing it well
Fashion & beauty brands
Presenting polished looks as if being assessed by Miranda, then cutting to chaotic reality
Workplace content creators
Using the dismissive tone to capture office dynamics and boss-employee relationships
FILM TIE-IN NOSTALGIA AUDIO TREND
TK-02
"Everything Hallelujah" Gratitude Format
Justin Bieber's acoustic track from Coachella became May's feel-good trend for listing small, ordinary gratitudes.
Creators film slice-of-life B-roll — coffee runs, sunsets, daily wins — with text overlays ending in "hallelujah." The format is gentle and earnest, working as both personal reflection and brand integration. Wellness brands and lifestyle creators are using it between promotional posts as a pressure-release valve.
Doing it well
Wellness & lifestyle creators
Weaving brand moments naturally into gratitude lists without forced promotion
Mental health advocates
Using the format to connect authentically with audiences seeking calm content
MOOD-DRIVEN BRAND-SAFE LOW PRODUCTION
TK-03
Subtle Foreshadowing Hook
Instead of long intros, creators add a 1-2 second flash at the start showing what happens later — instant hook, higher completion.
This editing technique solves TikTok's skip problem by front-loading intrigue. Bakers show the fail before the process, fitness creators flash the transformation before the workout. The algorithm rewards watch-through, and this format delivers it by making viewers stay to see how you get to that teaser moment.
Doing it well
Process creators (baking, DIY, fitness)
Showing the "money shot" upfront then rewinding to build the story
Educational accounts
Teasing the payoff (result, revelation) to retain attention through explanation
RETENTION HACK EDITING TECHNIQUE CROSS-NICHE
Platform 02
Instagram
IG-01
Bebot Transformation Trend
Set to "Bebot" by Black Eyed Peas, creators start casual then transition on the beat drop to reveal Filipino-inspired glam looks.
This beauty transformation format is climbing and hasn't peaked yet based on posting velocity. Non-Filipino creators are starting to adapt it as a general glam transformation. The format combines cultural pride with accessible beauty content, making it both specific and adaptable.
Doing it well
Filipino beauty creators
Leading the trend with authentic cultural celebration and makeup artistry
Mainstream beauty accounts
Adapting the beat-drop structure for general transformation content
TRANSFORMATION RISING AUDIO-DRIVEN
IG-02
Carousel Photo Dumps with Intentional Sequencing
Carousels are producing consistent follower growth when treated as visual stories with emotional arcs, not random photo collections.
Instagram now allows reordering slides after posting, removing the biggest pain point and accelerating carousel frequency. Creators are mixing selfies, scenery, food, candid moments, and memes in deliberate sequences. The format drives saves and shares — stronger algorithm signals than likes alone.
Doing it well
Lifestyle photographers
Curating dumps as mini photo essays with narrative flow and aesthetic consistency
Travel creators
Building location stories across 8-10 slides that feel cohesive, not cluttered
ALGORITHM FAVOURITE SAVES + SHARES FEATURE UPDATE
IG-03
"I Have Therapy" POV Format
Creators film themselves saying "sorry I can't, I have therapy" then cut to whatever actually restores them — the gym, baking, the beach.
This self-care reframe format is everywhere as audiences respond to content about mental health and boundary-setting. It's relatable, low-production, and works across niches. Brands can adapt it by positioning their product/service as the restorative "therapy" without being heavy-handed.
Doing it well
Wellness & mental health creators
Normalizing self-care rituals and showing real-life coping mechanisms
Fitness & hobby accounts
Reframing activities (running, art, cooking) as legitimate mental health practices
WELLNESS POV FORMAT HIGH RELATABILITY
Platform 03
LinkedIn
LI-01
Carousel Documents Dominating Engagement
Native PDFs and carousels lead all formats at 7% engagement — more than double the rate of standard video or single images.
Carousels earn 1,451 average impressions per post versus 605 for video. They increase dwell time, saves, and shares — signals LinkedIn's algorithm rewards heavily. High-performing carousels guide readers through one clear idea from start to finish: frameworks, breakdowns, or stories structured across slides.
Doing it well
B2B strategists & consultants
Packaging frameworks and playbooks into scannable, saveable carousel decks
Thought leaders in tech/marketing
Breaking down complex concepts into visual step-by-step narratives
TOP FORMAT 2026 HIGH IMPRESSIONS NATIVE CONTENT
LI-02
Clicks as the "Invisible Interaction"
Clicks rose 4.9% year-over-year and now drive engagement rates more than visible likes and comments — LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" tap.
Quality and clickability matter more than posting frequency. Engagement rates jumped from 12.21% to 13.90% even as weekly post volume dropped 10%. The takeaway: create content that earns interaction (polls, carousels, external links) rather than just scrolling past.
Doing it well
Data-driven marketers
Structuring posts with clear CTAs and link previews that invite clicks
Newsletter publishers
Using LinkedIn as traffic source to owned content with compelling teasers
ALGORITHM INSIGHT HIDDEN METRIC POST LESS, EARN MORE
LI-03
Short-Form Video Under 90 Seconds
Video under 90 seconds performs best as LinkedIn pushes native video harder in feeds — but text posts still lead at 51% user preference for brand content.
Video testimonials, 60-second tips, and behind-the-scenes clips work well for credibility and personality. Watch time increased 36% year-over-year. Balance is key: video should complement text-led strategy, not replace it. The platform rewards diverse content mixes, not single-format strategies.
Doing it well
Recruiters & HR professionals
Posting 60-second "top resume mistakes" or interview tip videos with captions
Service businesses
Using client testimonial videos and case study recaps for social proof
VIDEO STRATEGY UNDER 90 SEC PLATFORM PUSH
Platform 04
YouTube
YT-01
Serialized Formats & Recurring Shows
Creators are building "digital franchises" — recurring formats like Feeding Starving Celebrities and Coulda Been House that give viewers structured arcs and reasons to return.
These aren't one-off vlogs; they're episodic series with production value, recurring characters, and narrative continuity. Audiences crave structure and deeper value. Channels mixing this format with Shorts for discovery are outperforming Shorts-only or long-form-only strategies.
Doing it well
Quenlin Blackwell (Feeding Starving Celebrities)
Turning celebrity interviews into humor-driven recurring dinner series
Druski (Coulda Been House/Love)
Creating Big Brother-style reality franchises with limited-run seasons
CONTENT FRANCHISE EPISODIC BINGE APPEAL
YT-02
Long-Form + Shorts Hybrid Strategy
After years of Shorts dominance, watch time on 10+ minute videos is growing again — but Shorts remain essential for discovery.
Channels publishing both formats are outperforming single-format channels. Shorts drive discovery and reach; long-form drives watch time, ad revenue, and audience depth. The strongest 2026 channels treat them as complementary, not competitive. Shorts act as trailers for deeper content.
Doing it well
Educational creators (Gary's Economics, Madeline Argy)
Using Shorts for hooks/teasers, long-form for depth and subscriber loyalty
Gaming & commentary channels
Repurposing long-form highlights as Shorts to feed discovery funnel
DUAL-FORMAT MONETIZATION DISCOVERY FUNNEL
YT-03
Behind-the-Scenes "Build in Public" Series
Studios and creators are pulling audiences into production pipelines with casual, transparent BTS content — Glitch Direct is the standout example.
Glitch Studio launched a vlog-style format previewing upcoming animated series, blurring the line between creator and studio. Fans feel included in the creative process, building anticipation and community before launch. This transparency trend works for brands, agencies, and product teams willing to show unpolished process.
Doing it well
Glitch (Animation Studio)
Sharing pilots, concept art, and production updates in low-fi founder vlogs
Indie game developers
Documenting dev diaries and playtests to build pre-launch community
TRANSPARENCY COMMUNITY-LED BUILD IN PUBLIC
