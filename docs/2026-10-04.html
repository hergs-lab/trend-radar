TikTok
Educational formats, unpolished one-shots, and series structures dominate October
TK-01
"Someone's Gotta Hold It Down"
The person who stayed behind films the place everyone else left — no polish required.
This format celebrates the unglamorous work when others are at the event. One static shot, text on screen, raw and relatable. It resonates because audiences connect with being the person holding the fort.
Doing it well
Small business owners — filming the shop floor or inbox while the team is out
Service industry accounts — showing the kitchen at close when everyone else left early
Unpolished Relatable One-shot
TK-02
Educational How-To Content
Short explainers drive saves and rewatches — the two signals TikTok's algorithm values most.
Educational content surfaces in TikTok search long after posting, making it one of the few durable formats on a trend-heavy platform. How-to videos account for 16.1% of best-performing brand posts and compound over time through search.
Doing it well
Tutorial creators — answering specific "how to" queries directly, under 60 seconds
Product demo accounts — clear step-by-step walkthroughs optimized for search discovery
Search-friendly Saves Rewatches
TK-03
Series-Based Content
Part 1, Part 2, Part 3 structures give viewers a reason to come back — and TikTok rewards it.
Series content performs because it signals sustained interest to the algorithm. Viewers return for the next episode, creating consistent engagement patterns that increase visibility. Weekly themes and episodic formats are replacing one-off trend participation.
Doing it well
Story-driven creators — breaking narratives into multi-part formats with cliffhangers
Educational accounts — creating recurring weekly themes that build loyal viewership
Retention Episodic Loyal audience
Instagram
DM shares now outrank likes; fast-cut Reels and carousels lead engagement
IG-01
DM-First Content Design
Content people send privately carries more ranking weight than content they passively like.
Instagram head Adam Mosseri confirms DM shares (sends) are among the top three ranking signals, counting slightly more than likes for reaching non-followers. Build posts that make someone think "I know exactly who needs this."
Doing it well
@stormwfhmom — "My Reaction To" format creates shareable pay-scale ladders
B2B founders — product problem diagnoses that viewers forward to colleagues
DM shares Algorithm Reach
IG-02
Fast-Cut Reels (8-15 cuts/30 sec)
Rapid editing synced to music beats keeps viewers watching past the critical 3-second mark.
Fast-paced edits with strong hooks in the first two seconds are defining 2026 Reels. Creators time captions to music beats or voiceover pauses, creating rhythm that holds attention. Pattern interrupts at second 7 prevent drop-off.
Doing it well
Fashion and beauty creators — transformation reveals with beat-synced caption timing
Lifestyle brands — product showcases cut to trending audio with visual hooks
Retention Fast-cut Beat-sync
IG-03
Carousels Lead Engagement
Carousels achieve 0.50% engagement by followers — narrowly beating Reels at 0.48%.
While Reels generate wider reach for accounts under 50K followers, carousels consistently drive the highest engagement rate. Trial Reels feature allows A/B testing content with non-followers before grid publishing. The strategic mix: 60-70% Reels, 20-30% carousels.
Doing it well
Educational creators — multi-slide how-to guides that viewers swipe through completely
Design accounts — before/after sequences that reward the swipe
Engagement Dwell time Saves
LinkedIn
Native documents dominate; vulnerable storytelling outperforms polish
LI-01
Native Carousels (PDFs)
Native documents lead all formats with 7% engagement — a 14% year-over-year increase.
LinkedIn's 2026 benchmark data shows carousels generate 2-3x the engagement of standard text posts. High dwell time makes them a core ranking signal. Educational content in PDF carousel format compounds reach while establishing expertise.
Doing it well
B2B consultants — multi-slide frameworks that viewers save and reference later
Industry experts — data-driven insights packaged as scrollable visual stories
Engagement Dwell time Saves
LI-02
Vulnerable First-Person Stories
Posts about setbacks attract 8.5x the average engagement — honesty beats highlight reels.
Personal stories with business lessons consistently outperform generic advice. Research shows people are 22 times more likely to remember facts wrapped in story. Sharing challenges, not just wins, signals authenticity the algorithm rewards.
Doing it well
Founders — honest reflections on failed launches or hiring mistakes with lessons
Sales leaders — sharing difficult client conversations and what they learned
Authenticity Story-driven Comments
LI-03
Short-Form Native Video
Video under 90 seconds gets 7.2x more engagement than static posts — and it's shared 20x more.
LinkedIn reported three consecutive quarters of double-digit growth in video uploads. Video delivers highest engagement of all post types. Founders and executives using direct-to-camera talking head formats build credibility and show personality quickly.
Doing it well
CEOs and founders — unedited selfie-camera insights filmed in under 60 seconds
Industry commentators — timely reactions to news with clear point of view
Video Native Shares
YouTube
Shorts drive 61% of discovery; hybrid creators balance short and long formats
YT-01
Shorts as Discovery Engine
Over 61% of organic views come from Shorts feed — average watch time is 16 seconds.
YouTube Shorts generates over 200 billion daily views, making it the primary first-contact format. Successful creators use Shorts for discovery, then funnel engaged viewers to long-form content. Open with the problem or claim immediately — no branding intros.
Doing it well
Educational creators — 15-second fact hooks that tease full explanations in long videos
DIY channels — before/after transformations that drive curiosity for full tutorials
Discovery Shorts Hook
YT-02
TV-Style Serialized Formats
Creators embracing traditional TV formats with hour-long episodes — serialization builds loyal audiences.
YouTube's 2026 Culture Report highlights nostalgic, serialized content gaining traction. Creators like Quenlin Blackwell's "Feeding Starving Celebrities" have grown from 10-minute to hour-long episodes. Viewers spend 40 minutes per session on average, rewarding depth.
Doing it well
Quenlin Blackwell — "Feeding Starving Celebrities" serialized celebrity cooking show
Documentary-style creators — multi-episode series with consistent posting schedules
Long-form Series Watch time
YT-03
Hybrid Creator Strategy
Short-form for reach, long-form for monetization — the strategic split is now standard.
Viewers watch Shorts to discover creators, then commit to long-form to understand them deeply. Long-form remains the monetization powerhouse with higher CPMs. Successful creators balance both: Shorts for cold audiences, 8-20 minute videos for subscribers.
Doing it well
Educational YouTubers — Shorts teasing full deep-dive videos with chapter timestamps
Commentary channels — viral Shorts clips driving viewers to 15+ minute analysis videos
Hybrid Funnel Monetization
