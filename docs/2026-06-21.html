TikTok

TK-01
2026 Summer Anthem
Seven-second lip-sync to Josh Fawaz's "Like a Prayer" remix — no concept required, just camera and text overlay.
Small creators are reporting millions of views on first attempts. The Madonna sample delivers instant nostalgia and emotional pull, removing every barrier to participation. The format is so low-lift that it's fueling a viral snowball, with the FYP treating it as free money.
Doing it well
First-time creators (11K-100K accounts)
Small accounts hitting 5M+ views by keeping it simple: lip-sync, text, post
Brand founders (product personification angle)
"When our serum sees a Tuesday morning" — low-lift brand participation
Peak volume Audio-led Discovery driver
TK-02
Rock Music Glitch Edit
Charli XCX's "Rock Music" glitch becomes an edit mechanic — freeze the frame mid-motion using Instagram's stuck-frame animation.
The deliberate vocal malfunction mid-song is the whole format. Creators film a clip, sync to the glitch section, then apply the stuck frame animation so it holds while audio keeps moving. Works for product reveals, fit checks, food pours — anything worth lingering on. Intermediate-level editing but high shareability.
Doing it well
Fashion & food content creators
Using the freeze for outfit transitions and iced coffee pours with perfect timing
Product-focused brands
Freeze-frame on product highlight moment before attention drops (3-5 sec clips)
Edit-driven Cross-platform High retention
TK-03
AI Absurdism (Bombardiro Crocodilo)
AI-generated animals in bizarre roles (crocodile-plane hybrids, Italian text-to-speech voiceovers) are flooding feeds daily.
Videos featuring AI-generated animals have taken over, with brands recognizing viral potential fast. RyanAir's Spionero Golubiro execution is a perfect case study. The trend works because it leans into absurdist humor and "brainrot" content that Gen Z and Gen Alpha find irresistible. Low production barrier using AI tools.
Doing it well
RyanAir (@ryanair)
Spionero Golubiro execution — brand absurdism done right with fast cultural response
Meme-native brands
Using existing AI animals and integrating into product narratives with Italian voiceover
AI-generated Absurdist humor Brand-friendly

Instagram

IG-01
Algorithm Preference Test
Pit a high-effort edit against a deadpan static shot in the same Reel — let the algorithm and comments decide which performs.
The "Seeing if the algorithm prefers" format is two weeks deep and still climbing. Creators are using it to test creative hypotheses while driving comment engagement. No specific audio required, making it fully accessible to business accounts. The meta-commentary angle makes audiences feel involved in the creative process.
Doing it well
@itslifewithp
7.5K likes, 1.6K comments testing polished vs. raw — textbook execution
Small business accounts
Using format to test product shots vs. founder-led content with audience input
Format-driven No audio barrier Engagement bait
IG-02
Trial Reels as Testing Ground
Instagram's Trial Reel feature lets creators A/B test content with non-followers before publishing to main grid.
This is less a consumer trend and more a creator-ops shift. It changes how creators test hooks before committing to the main grid. The strongest examples are educational and direct-to-camera. Creators are using it to validate content performance with cold audiences before risking grid real estate, treating it as a strategic safeguard.
Doing it well
Education-focused creators
Testing new hooks on non-followers, checking 72-hour performance before grid commit
Brand content teams
Using Trials to validate messaging with cold audiences before campaign launch
Creator tool Risk mitigation Workflow shift
IG-03
Everything Hallelujah Listing Format
Justin Bieber's "Everything Hallelujah" continues dominating brand-friendly listing Reels — products, tips, favorites.
One of the strongest list-format trends of the month. The audio works for social media managers, restaurants, fitness studios, e-commerce — any niche with listable value. Format is simple: overlay text listing items while audio plays. High saves, strong evergreen potential, and commercially friendly since it's native to creator accounts.
Doing it well
Product-based brands (DTC, e-comm)
Listing favorite products, top sellers, or gift guides with simple text overlays
Service businesses (studios, agencies)
Showcasing offerings, tools, or client results in list format with high save rate
List format Brand-safe High saves

LinkedIn

LI-01
Carousels Dominate Engagement
Native carousel/PDF posts lead all formats with 7% engagement rate — 17x more interactions than single images.
Multi-image posts boast 3.71% engagement, more than double standard video (1.80%) or single images (1.81%). Carousels earn 1,451 impressions per post on average, far surpassing video's 605. The algorithm rewards formats that encourage on-platform interaction (swipes, clicks). Users post images 6x more than carousels, creating a massive opportunity gap.
Doing it well
B2B SaaS founders
Turning blog insights into 5-7 slide carousels with data visualizations and takeaways
Thought leaders & consultants
Using carousel format for frameworks, case studies, and step-by-step breakdowns
Highest engagement Algorithm favored Underused
LI-02
Clicks Over Likes
Between 2025 and 2026, average clicks per post rose 4.90% — invisible engagement now drives authority more than visible likes.
LinkedIn tracks every carousel swipe and "see more" click. These actions factor into engagement rate even when users don't comment or like. The algorithm interprets clicks as quality signals. Average engagement jumped from 12.21% to 13.90% even as posting frequency dropped 10%. Quality and "clickability" now matter more than volume.
Doing it well
Long-form storytellers
Writing 1,200+ character posts with "see more" hooks that drive click-throughs
Data-driven marketers
Using carousels and external links strategically to maximize invisible engagement
Invisible metric Authority signal Quality over volume
LI-03
Raw Behind-the-Scenes Video
Unpolished, horizontal 3-6 minute clips outlining thought processes or frameworks are driving higher engagement than polished corporate content.
Audiences are starved for simple, helpful information delivered in a non-"thought-leadery" way. Video content generates 5x more engagement than static posts, but authenticity trumps production value. Native LinkedIn videos outperform YouTube links by 312%. Videos under 90 seconds perform best, but longer deep-dives (3-6 min) build credibility.
Doing it well
Agency founders & consultants
Filming casual walkthroughs of frameworks, unscripted and real, no heavy editing
B2B service providers
Sharing client case studies as 60-90 sec video testimonials with clear CTAs
Authenticity-first Video surge Native upload

YouTube

YT-01
Shorts-First Storytelling
Shorts dominate discovery, but the smartest creators use them as funnels to long-form, memberships, and products — not standalone content.
Shorts crossed 70 billion daily views. Creators earn $0.03-$0.10 per 1,000 Shorts views (vs. ~$3 RPM for long-form), but the volume potential is massive. The real story is the funnel: Shorts act as discovery mechanisms feeding long-form subscriptions. Channels mixing both formats outperform Shorts-only channels significantly.
Doing it well
Educational & tutorial creators
Using Shorts to tease long-form deep-dives, driving subscribers and watch time
Product reviewers & unboxers
Shorts for product teasers, long-form for detailed reviews and affiliate conversions
Discovery engine Funnel mechanic Dual-format strategy
YT-02
Episodic/Serialized Content
Creators embracing TV-style serialized formats — recurring characters, story arcs, binge-worthy episodes — are seeing massive subscriber growth.
YouTube's 2026 Culture & Trends Report highlights nostalgia for traditional TV formats. Druski's "Coulda Been House" reality franchise attracted 710K viewers. Quenlin Blackwell's "feeding starving celebrities" series became a recurring hit. Viewers prefer channels offering structured arcs and deeper value over one-off content. Limited series (under 10 episodes) perform best — enough for binge appeal without fatigue.
Doing it well
Druski (@druski)
"Coulda Been House" reality franchise — Big Brother format with creator chaos
Quenlin Blackwell
"Feeding starving celebrities" — humor-driven interview series with recurring format
Serialized Binge-worthy Subscriber retention
YT-03
Authenticity Over Polish
Creators are ditching highly-produced videos for authentic, unfiltered content — simpler thumbnails, relaxed pacing, sincere storytelling.
Thumbnails are getting simpler, fancy edits are disappearing. The goal is creating intimacy with viewers, not impressing them with production value. Sam Sulek (bodybuilder) and Dry Creek Wrangler School (horse channel turned life lessons) exemplify the shift. Audiences want personality-led authenticity over scripted perfection. The format signals trust and relatability in an AI-saturated landscape.
Doing it well
Sam Sulek (bodybuilding)
Unfiltered gym vlogs with minimal editing — raw personality drives millions of views
Dry Creek Wrangler School
Pivoted from horse training to uncut life lesson videos — sincere over polished
Unpolished Personality-led Anti-overproduction
